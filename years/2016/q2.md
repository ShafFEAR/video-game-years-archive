### Chameleon Run

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co2shu.jpg" alt="Chameleon Run cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** Android, iOS, tvOS, Windows Phone, Nintendo Switch, Windows &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Hyperbolic Magnetism &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** Noodlecake Studios &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Action &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** April 7, 2016

> Chameleon Run is a single player game primarily available to mobile devices. The game is a high speed auto-runner with a satisfying double-jump mechanic played in landscape mode with both hands.    The game features minimal, yet colourful aesthetics, with upbeat music and nice sound effects. There are no virtual controls visible on screen, instead the player taps on either side of the display to control the avatar.    The goal of the game is to pick up items while traversing the track to reach the finish line. The items picked up are used to unlock more tracks. Reaching the goal will require the avatar to jump over obstacles while at all times ensuring that the colour of the avatar matches the colour of the running surface. The latter mechanic is what makes this runner somewhat unique. Many of the tracks feature two or more paths to the goal, which increases the longevity of the game.    First launch opens directly on the first track, which is used to teach how to switch colour mid-air while jumping to prevent the avatar from exploding. After the tutorial is completed, you are taken to the menu where the first track is unlocked.    The game consists of a series of grouped tracks which have to be unlocked by collecting enough chameleons - found along the tracks. The number of chameleons required to progress is shown on screen while in the menu.    As of writing the game is available to iOS, Android and Windows mobile phones, as well as tvOS (Apple TV).
>
> _Synopsis source: IGDB_

[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon_Run) · [IGDB](https://www.igdb.com/games/chameleon-run)

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co223s.jpg" alt="Until I Have You cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** Windows, Linux &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Wormwood Studios &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** Digital Tribe &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Side-scrolling, action, platform &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** April 4, 2016

> Until I Have You is a 2D side-scrolling action platform video game which has 12 chapters with 4 stages and a unique boss each, taking place within a different setting. The goal for each stage is to get to the end of the stage as fast as possible without falling, getting killed or depleting your sanity gauge.  Central to Until I Have You is the exoskeleton suit. The suit gives the player special abilities at the cost of the player's sanity. The exoskeleton comes with the abilities to slow down time, ala Max Payne and to rewind time. However, overuse of these abilities will cause the sanity bar to deplete making your character lose touch with reality. When the sanity bar depletes your character starts to hallucinate, altering gameplay mechanics and level design.  In each stage, the player navigates his way from left to right through platforming sections, death traps, and enemies. The goal is to do this as fast as possible with the least amount of deaths. The fast pace of the game can make it difficult to plan a perfect approach. To make up for this, the game allows the player to restart each stage the moment they die, allowing them to quickly fine-tune their approach over several attempts. At the end of each chapter the player must also defeat a unique boss. Along the way the player acquires a number of weapons and upgrades that you can cycle through, all of which have a specific use.  The game grades the player's performance at the end of each stage based on a number of factors and given a percentage raring out of 100. Things you get judged on include method of death, whether you kill any of the enemies and what you killed them with.
>
