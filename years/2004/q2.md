### Ballance

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co9trv.jpg" alt="Ballance cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** Windows &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Cyparade &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** Atari Europe, Ziggurat Interactive &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Puzzle &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** April 2, 2004

> In Ballance, you control a sphere with the simple objective of navigating it to the end of each of the game's 12 linear levels. Expect surreal environments as you transform between three different materials to push boxes, spheres, and other objects to clear a path towards the end. Pre-placed "Transformers" will turn your ball into either Paper, Wood, or Stone, each with their own downsides and advantages.  Paper can, for example, use gusts of wind to reach higher elevations, fall slower than the other materials, and get around quickly. Stone may be required to push heavy materials, but, due to its immense weight, will experience different issues when trying to navigate environments. Wood is the neutral material of choice: it rolls reliably and quickly, allowing it to stick to sideways railing while still being able to push objects of a medium density.
>
> _Synopsis source: IGDB_

### Chaos League

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co2gb1.jpg" alt="Chaos League cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** Windows &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Cyanide &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** Focus Home Interactive, Digital Jesters, Cyanide &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Sports, management, real-time strategy &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** June 25, 2004

> Chaos League is a fantasy-based sports management game developed by Cyanide Studios and published by Digital Jesters. It was released in Europe on 8 August 2004 and later in North America on 8 March 2005. The game is a spin on American football, the violence of the Medieval football with no rules and rugby-style of sports yet set in a fantasy world with teams being made up of fantasy races such as dwarves, elves, orcs and undead, along with the use of magic and other fictional elements during a "match". The tone of the game is satirical with comedic color commentary and adverts for fictional in-game universe products. An official expansion was later released in 2005 bundled with the original, titled Chaos League: Sudden Death that added new features and gameplay tweaks.    While not a direct video game adaption, Chaos League bears resemblance to Games Workshop's Blood Bowl tabletop board game, which is also about fantasy sports. Cyanide Studios would later develop the video game version of Blood Bowl in 2009, which itself utilizes certain features from Chaos League.
>
> _Synopsis source: IGDB_

### Cube 2: Sauerbraten

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co2hqh.jpg" alt="Cube 2: Sauerbraten cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** Windows, Linux, macOS &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Wouter van Oortmerssen, Lee Salzman, Mike Dysart &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** First-person shooter &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** May 6, 2004

> Cube 2: Sauerbraten (German for "sour roast", also known as Sauer) is a cross-platform, Quake-like first-person shooter that runs on Microsoft Windows, Linux, FreeBSD and Mac OS X using OpenGL and SDL. The game features single-player and multiplayer gameplay and contains an in-game level editor. The game engine is free and open-source software, under the zlib License, with commercial support available from the developer's own business counterpart, Dot3 Labs. The game media is released under various non-free licenses. The aim of the project is not to produce the most features and highest-quality graphics possible, but rather to allow map-editing to be done in real-time within the game, while keeping the engine source code small and elegant.
>
