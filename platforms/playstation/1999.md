### 70's Robot Anime Geppy-X

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co5wmv.jpg" alt="70's Robot Anime Geppy-X cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** PlayStation &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Aroma &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** Aroma &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Horizontal-scrolling shooter &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** May 27, 1999

> Geppy-X is a 2D side-scrolling shoot-em-up developed as an affectionate parody of the 1970s super robot era of mecha anime pioneered by Go Nagai and Ken Ishikawa. In it, you play as a super robot fighter squadron in an alternate universe 1970s, fighting against alien invaders from the Space Devil Empire.  The titular robot has three transformations all modeled after famous 1970s super robots, as well as a super form known as Geppy-XX. Stages are presented like actual anime episodes, and the game makes use of the PlayStation's full motionn video capabilities to include actual animations made specifically for the game, with an opening, part A, eye-catch, part B, ending theme, and next episode preview. They even have fake commercials that play in between the eye-catches.

> Ace Combat 3: Electrosphere is a combat flight simulation video game developed and published by Namco for the PlayStation. ### Action Bass

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co6v5q.jpg" alt="Action Bass cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** PlayStation &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Vingt-et-un Systems Corporation &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** Syscom, Take-Two Interactive &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Fishing &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** December 9, 1999

> Fishing is usually a relaxing sport, even in videogame form. Action Bass attempts to introduce people to the world of fishing, and comes in four play modes. In Challenge mode, you only get three minutes to catch a fish large enough to meet the weight requirement and place you within the top three fishermen. When you pass the first level, you'll have three other levels to defeat. Beating all four levels unlocks new lures and grants access to the Extra option.  The second play mode in Action Bass is the Free mode, where you can freely fish in any of the four available areas and hone your fishing skills. Here you can discover the best spots to catch the bigger fish in less time in preparation for the Challenge mode. You also have the option of saving your fish for the Aquarium, the third mode in Action Bass, where the fish you caught in the Free mode can be viewed by moving in all directions around the fish.

### Bass Landing

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/ilhrolte4trkjsjxdv8m.jpg" alt="Bass Landing cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** PlayStation &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Tose &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** ASCII Entertainment, Agetec &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Sports &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** January 14, 1999

> Designed for the armchair angler in mind, Bass Landing captures the essence of being on an actual lake and boat without the hassle of reaching into your tackle box and pulling back a handful of slime! There are five different lakes in all, ranging from the murky Lunker Lake to the mountainous clarity of Crystal Lake. Each contains natural and manmade hazards (branches, poles, rocks) and different kind of fish--depending on the kind of fish you want, choose your lake accordingly.  For instance, the Silver Carp can only be found in dark, murky lakes or swamps and the Spotted Bass resides in the chilly, colder water areas. While the name of the game is bass, there are 14 different types of fish including the small but feisty Blue Gill, massively dynamic Carp, and the multiplying Rainbow Trout.  In addition to his or her game, the armchair fisherman will need to know how his fishing rod works and what lures to use on the various fish. There are fifty different tools in all ranging from Spinner Bait to the MHB-665 Bait Rod--one will want to know the functions of each lure and rig and choose an appropriate rod for the type of fish being hunted.
>
> _Synopsis source: IGDB_

### Zill O'll

<img src="https://images.igdb.com/igdb/image/upload/t_cover_big/co4o35.jpg" alt="Zill O'll cover" width="200">

**Platforms:** PlayStation, PlayStation 2 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Developer:** Koei &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Publisher:** Koei &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Genre:** Role-playing &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Released:** October 7, 1999

> Zill O'll is a nonlinear fantasy role-playing game set on the continent of Vyashion. Following the fall of Emperor Balor, a fragile peace settles over the land as a new threat begins to emerge with the awakening of the Dragon King. Players create their own protagonist and choose from different starting scenarios before setting out on an open-ended adventure. The game's free-scenario system allows events to unfold according to the player's actions and the passage of time, affecting relationships, the fate of major characters, political conflicts and the eventual ending. Combat is turn-based, while character development revolves around the game's Soul system.
>
